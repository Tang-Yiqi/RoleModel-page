01 / THE IDEA

A teacher can do
more than predict.

Conventional output distillation aligns a student’s forecasts with a teacher’s outputs on observed histories. RoleModel asks a different question: what if the teacher could create better examples to learn from?

A forward teacher predicts the future; a backward teacher reconstructs the history. Together, they generate new history–future pairs. The student selects useful, non-redundant supervision as it learns.

Roll to synthesize

Alternate two frozen teachers to expand both historical inputs and their forecasting targets beyond the original samples.

Select for the student

Combine teacher–student discrepancy with sample diversity, prioritizing unlearned knowledge without repeating similar examples.

Keep the interface simple

Transfer knowledge through sequence outputs. No representation alignment, no extra student parameters, no teacher at inference time.

Why does output alignment broaden student compatibility?

Representation-based students learn representations before prediction. Direct-mapping students such as DLinear, NLinear, and SparseTSF produce forecasts without a separately learned representation. Both support output alignment, while only Representation-based students provide representations for alignment.

02 / THE METHOD

RoleModelRoll the teachers.
Grow the supervision.

RoleModel combines bidirectional rolling sample synthesis with student-aware sample selection. Its three stages train forward and backward teachers, synthesize history–future pairs, and select informative samples for output alignment.

STEP 01 · TEACHER TRAINING

Learn the relationship in both directions.

Train the forward teacher ℱ to map history X to future Y, and the backward teacher ℬ to map Y back to X. Both learn from original training pairs, and both are frozen before synthesis.

Forwardℱ : X → Y
Backwardℬ : Y → X

Original pairs supervise both teachers. Neither teacher is updated during distillation.

Different roles, one forecasting task. The backward teacher expands the historical inputs. The forward teacher provides targets for history-to-future prediction.

03 / THE LIGHTWEIGHT STUDENT

Lambs(Lightweight And MLP Based Student)

Designed specifically for the RoleModel distillation framework.

A channel-dependent main branch captures multivariate context. A channel-independent residual branch preserves each variable’s own history. Together, they give a lightweight student the capacity to learn from its teachers.

01

Embed across channels

Jointly embed all variables at each time step into a compact hidden vector.

ht = MLPemb(Xt,:)
02

Aggregate through time

A shared MLP recursively combines neighboring nodes into one global representation.

hparent = MLPagg([hleft; hright])
03

Keep each channel distinct

Add the global root to each channel’s residual, then apply the shared forecasting head.

Ŷ:,c = Predictor(g + rc)

More teachers.

Homogeneous specialists or heterogeneous architectures can supply a single combined sequence. Rolling synthesis and student training stay unchanged.

07 / WHAT MAKES IT WORK?

Better examples.
A student ready to learn.

Ablations separate the value of sample selection from the capacity of the student. Both sides matter for effective knowledge transfer.

01

Discrepancy and diversity
are complementary.

Teacher–student discrepancy prioritizes samples that remain challenging, while sample diversity favors supervision different from the selected samples. Together, they tailor supervision to the student’s learning state; removing either criterion degrades performance. Including backward-synthesized samples can also degrade performance: the backward teacher expands historical inputs, while the forward teacher provides forecasting targets.

its role under distillation.

Moderate dropout helps standalone training in this experiment, but reduces accuracy under RoleModel. Rich synthetic supervision rewards retaining the student’s fitting capacity.

THE TAKEAWAY

Improve the supervision.
Keep the student lightweight.

RoleModel shifts the focus from aligning hidden representations to creating and selecting the right forecasting examples.
